# Copy and content

Interface copy is part of the design. Short, plain, specific, and true.

## Rules

- **Say the concrete thing.** "See which invoices are overdue" beats "Unlock smarter insights".
- **One idea per sentence.** Cut words until the meaning breaks, then add one back.
- **Plain language** a 12-year-old understands. No jargon unless your users use it daily.
- **Name things the same way everywhere**, including emails and notifications.
- **Buttons are verbs** that say what happens: "Send invoice", "Save changes", not "Submit", "OK".
- **Errors** say what happened and what to do: "That link has expired. We sent you a new one." Never blame the user.
- **Empty states** say what will appear and how to make it appear.
- **No promises the product cannot keep.** No guarantees, no invented numbers, no "#1" claims you cannot prove.
- **Don't over-explain.** If a footnote or tooltip is needed to understand a label, fix the label.
- **Numbers and counts** in labels must match what is shown, every time.
- **Write for the reader's moment.** On a phone at a busy moment, two seconds of attention is all you get.

## AI-sounding filler to cut

Phrases that make copy read machine-written. Delete them or say the specific thing instead.

- "Level up", "game changer", "supercharge", "unlock", "unleash", "elevate", "seamless", "effortless", "revolutionise", "next-level"
- "Not just X, it's Y" and "No X. No Y. Just Z." constructions
- "In today's fast-paced world", "Whether you're a ... or a ..."
- "Plain words", "No guesswork", "No fluff" (describing the copy instead of being it)
- Triplets for rhythm ("Fast. Simple. Powerful.") when the three words say nothing specific
- Excessive em dashes, emoji as bullet points, exclamation marks in UI text
- Hedge stacks: "may potentially help you to possibly"

## House style template

Record these in the project's design-system doc so every contributor writes the same way:

- Sentence case or title case for headings and buttons (pick one).
- Dashes and punctuation preferences.
- Banned words for the brand (for legal, tone or audience reasons).
- How to name competitors, partners and third-party brands (each by its own name, spelled the way they spell it).
- Local spelling (US or UK) and number, date and currency formats.
